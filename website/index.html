  • Web Push reminders on any device you opt in, delivered even when the app is closed

Savings & goals

Piggies for saving, Capitals for planning

Two purpose-built tools for money you're setting aside — piggy banks for open-ended savings goals, and Capitals plans for big one-off expenses with a real budget and a deadline.

  • Piggies hold unassigned savings — every savings category becomes a goal with a balance, an optional target date, and a progress ring; subcategories show as piglets inside it
  • When adding a savings deposit, optionally assign it to a Capitals plan — it shows as Saved on that plan instead of in Piggies
  • Saving Insights surface streaks, best months, savings rate, and pace-vs-deadline projections
  • Capitals count as saving too — assigned deposits feed your rate, net, and streak, and every plan shows what is set aside, what is left, and whether it is funded, on pace, or behind
  • Withdraw from a piggy without it being mistaken for income anywhere in the app
  • Capitals plans big one-off expenses — marriages, trips, loans — from a template or a blank plan
  • Check off Capitals line items as paid and log the payment straight into your ledger
  • A total budget per plan drives a monthly-save target: (unpaid budget − pot remaining) ÷ months left

Privacy & identity

The server sees ciphertext. That's it.

Amounts, wallet names, categories, notes, schedule titles, budget holds, and to-dos are AES-256-GCM encrypted in your browser before anything reaches the database. Only day-level dates needed for accurate reminders stay in plaintext — plus, on events where you switch reminders on, a readable copy of what that email or push notification says. Everything else is unreadable server-side.

  • Sign in with a Web3 wallet — create or restore with a 12- or 24-word recovery phrase, no email or password
  • Device passphrase vault wraps your keys locally with PBKDF2 + AES-GCM instead of plaintext storage
  • Encrypted backup you can download and restore, encrypted with your own ledger key — never stored server-side
  • Revoke devices, clear local data, and manage third-party data-sharing consent anytime
  • In-app Transparency view maps exactly what the server can and can't infer, with top-to-bottom diagrams

FAQ

Questions worth asking before you trust an app with your money

What Custos encrypts, what it still needs in plaintext, and what you give up in exchange.

Is Custos really end-to-end encrypted?

Yes. Amounts, wallet names, categories, notes, schedule titles, budget holds, and to-dos are encrypted with AES-256-GCM in your browser before anything reaches the database, so the server only ever stores ciphertext it cannot read. Two deliberate exceptions stay in plaintext: day-level dates, which are needed to fire reminders on time, and — only on events where you switch reminders on — a readable copy of what that email or push notification will say.

Do I need an email address or a password?

No. You sign in with an in-browser Web3 wallet, created or restored from a 12- or 24-word recovery phrase, using a cryptographic challenge instead of credentials. There is no signup form, no password to reuse, and no email address on file unless you switch on email reminders yourself.

Do I need to buy crypto or pay gas fees?

No. The wallet is used purely as an identity key to sign in and derive your encryption key — Custos never sends a transaction, never touches a blockchain, and never asks you to fund anything. You can use a ledger-only key so your sign-in address is not correlated with any on-chain activity.

How is this different from other budgeting apps?

Most expense trackers keep your data in plaintext on their servers and ask for an email address, a password, and often a bank connection. Custos asks for none of that: no signup form, no bank linking, and no readable server-side copy of your spending. What you trade for it is recovery — the key is yours alone.

Is Custos free?

Yes — every feature is free on the official hosted app. The source is public for transparency under a proprietary license, which means it is not free to self-host, rebrand, or offer as a competing service; those uses need a written commercial agreement.

What happens if I lose my recovery phrase?

There is no recovery. Because your ledger is encrypted with a key only you hold, nobody — the operator included — can restore access or read your data. Keep the phrase somewhere safe, set a device passphrase so the key is wrapped on each browser, and download an encrypted backup from Account → Exports & imports.

Can I install Custos on my phone?

Yes. It is an installable PWA — add it to your home screen on Android or iOS and it runs like a native app, with Web Push reminders delivered even when it is closed. An IndexedDB ciphertext cache keeps your ledger readable offline; writing new entries still needs a connection.

Which currencies are supported, and how does conversion work?

You can create wallets in any of 29 currencies and run several side by side, each with its own budgets and categories. Insights converts across them using live rates so cross-wallet totals stay comparable, while individual entries always keep the currency you recorded them in.

Does dark mode stay readable?

Yes. The app follows your system preference (or a manual toggle) and the dark palette is tuned for WCAG 2.1 AA contrast — at least 4.5:1 for normal text and 3.0:1 for large text and UI chrome. Secondary labels, borders, filled buttons, and status fills are brightened so the ledger stays easy to scan in low light or glare.
